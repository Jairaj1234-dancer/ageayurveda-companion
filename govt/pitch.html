The public-mission premise

Ayurveda's classical knowledge needs
a scientific AI layer.
Generic models cannot provide it.

Today, large language models routinely fabricate Ayurvedic citations, conflate sources, and recommend formulations that the classical texts never sanctioned. The risk to public trust — and to a five-thousand-year tradition — is real, and it is happening at scale.

I · The risk
Generic AI hallucinates Ayurveda.

Ask a leading commercial chatbot what Charaka 5.7 says — it will confidently invent a verse. Brand chatbots and storefront assistants routinely cite passages that don't exist. That risk is now in millions of consumer interactions per day.

II · The gap
No standardised, citable, machine-readable corpus.

The Sanskrit mūla pāṭha is in the public domain, but no party has packaged it as a structured, verse-tagged retrieval surface that any AI system can use as ground-truth. CCRAS's recent MoUs with Berhampur University and Anuvadini AI are pointing in this direction.

III · The moment
The technology is finally good enough.

Multilingual large language models with citation discipline ship to production today. The IndiaAI Mission has stood up the compute. The AYUSH Grid and NAMASTE portal are establishing the digital scaffolding. The pieces have arrived simultaneously.

What we will deliver to the public

Four artefacts. Open-licensed. Useful to the country.

01
Structured-XML digital corpus, open-licensed.
Selected sthānas of Aṣṭāṅga Hṛdaya, Charaka Saṃhitā, Suśruta Saṃhitā, and Bhāvaprakāśa — each verse tagged with chapter, verse-range, semantic concept, and CCRAS-validated provenance. A national research artefact independent of any commercial product.
Form · Open-licensed dataset
02
Benchmark dataset for classical-text question-answering.
500 reference questions paired with verse-level ground-truth answers, validated by CCRAS- nominated scholars. A reference standard against which any future Ayurvedic AI system can be measured — comparable in spirit to the BODH initiative recently launched by NHA and IIT-Kanpur.
Form · Public benchmark
03
Provenance-validation toolkit, open-source.
A library that any researcher, brand, or clinic can use to independently verify whether an Ayurvedic claim is grounded in classical text. Strips the asymmetry between authoritative sources and commercial AI systems that too often invent them.
Form · Apache 2.0 software
04
Joint scholarly outputs.
Two co-authored publications in the partnership year — one technical (retrieval methodology and multilingual evaluation), one Ayurvedic-scholarly (verse-selection methodology and provenance convention). Joint authorship on every consequential dataset or tool release.
Form · Peer-reviewed papers
